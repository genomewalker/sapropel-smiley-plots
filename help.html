Help

The explorer shows one record per sample and reference genome. Metagenomic reads from each sediment sample were mapped competitively against bacterial, archaeal and viral reference genomes; for each genome with enough reads, metaDMG estimated the post-mortem damage and filterBAM summarised how the reads cover the genome.

Only records that passed the read-count and coverage filters of the analysis are included: normalised entropy above the value in config.json, breadth above the value in config.json and a breadth to expected-breadth ratio of at least the value in config.json. The values are stored in config.json.

Sample names combine the core and the depth interval, for example GeoB15103-1_19-34cm is 19–34 cm in core GeoB15103-1. Ages are in thousands of years (ka). The sample list is on the front page.

Smiley plot

After an organism dies, cytosines in the single-stranded overhangs at the ends of its DNA fragments deaminate to uracil, which is sequenced as thymine. Reads from old DNA therefore carry C→T substitutions near the 5′ end and, on the complementary strand, G→A substitutions near the 3′ end. The plot shows the frequency of these two substitutions against position in the read.

  • Left half. C→T frequency at positions 1–25 counted from the 5′ end. Circles, in orange-red.
  • Right half. G→A frequency at positions 25–1 counted from the 3′ end, mirrored so that the read end is at the right edge. Squares, in blue.
  • Markers are the observed frequencies reported by metaDMG: the substitution count relative to the number of reference C (or G) bases at that position.
  • Lines are the metaDMG fit.
  • Vertical axis. Shared by both halves. It extends to at least 35% so that weak profiles are not stretched to look strong.

A damaged profile is highest at position 1 on both sides and falls towards the read interior, which gives the plot its name. A flat profile at the background level indicates little or no damage. Profiles from genomes with few reads are noisy; read the fit together with its significance.

In the full view, pointing at the plot reads out the values at that position, and Plotted values lists all of them as a table.

Damage fit

metaDMG models the substitution frequency at position x from the read end as an exponential decay on a constant background:

f(x) = A · (1 − q)x − 1 + c

The substitution counts at each position are modelled as beta-binomial with concentration φ.

Damage damage
The amplitude A of the fitted curve, bias-corrected in the analysis pipeline (column A_b). It is set to 0 when the fit is flagged bad. Range 0–1; the explorer shows it as a percentage.
Significance significance
metaDMG's Z value: the damage estimate divided by its standard deviation. Larger values mean the estimate is further from zero relative to its uncertainty.
Fit fit
good or bad, as flagged in the analysis pipeline.
A, amplitude A_b
Excess substitution frequency at position 1 above the background.
q, decay q_b
Rate at which the excess falls with each position. Larger values confine the damage to the first few positions.
c, background c_b
Substitution frequency away from the read ends.
φ, concentration phi_b
Concentration of the beta-binomial distribution. Smaller values mean more scatter between positions than binomial sampling alone would give.

Damage status

A record is labelled damaged when both conditions hold:

  • the fit is not flagged bad, and
  • damage is greater than the threshold in config.json ().

All other records are labelled non-damaged. The threshold is the third quartile of the damage values of genomes with a good fit in the reference sample named in config.json, as computed by the analysis pipeline. It is read from config.json (damage_threshold) and used for the badges, the sunburst, the filters and every count in the explorer.

Non-damaged is a statement about the estimate, not about the age of the organism: a genome with few reads or a poor fit can fall below the threshold whatever its origin.

Status is never shown by colour alone. Badges are filled for damaged and outlined for non-damaged and carry the word; in the sunburst, outer segments are solid for damaged and hatched for non-damaged.

Coverage statistics

These columns come from filterBAM and describe how the mapped reads are distributed along each reference genome. They help to tell a genome that is present from one that only attracts reads to a few conserved or mobile regions.

Reads n_reads
Number of reads assigned to the genome.
Mean coverage coverage_mean
Mean sequencing depth over all positions of the reference.
Breadth breadth
Fraction of reference positions covered by at least one read.
Expected breadth exp_breadth
Breadth expected if the same reads were placed at random: 1 − e−mean coverage.
Breadth / expected breadth_exp_ratio
Observed breadth divided by expected breadth. Values near 1 are consistent with reads spread across the whole genome; low values mean the reads pile up in a few regions.
Normalised entropy norm_entropy
Entropy of the read distribution along the genome, scaled to 0–1. Higher values mean more even coverage.
Normalised Gini norm_gini
Gini coefficient of the coverage distribution, scaled to 0–1. Higher values mean more uneven coverage.
Coverage evenness cov_evenness
Evenness of coverage as computed by filterBAM. Higher values mean more even coverage.
Read ANI read_ani_mean
Mean nucleotide identity between the reads and the reference, in per cent.
Read length read_length_mean
Mean length of the mapped reads, in bases.

Explorer

Sample list

Samples are ordered by age, youngest first, like a core log. A dark band marks a sapropel layer and a pale hatched band marks non-sapropel sediment. Each entry gives the number of reference genomes and the share labelled damaged. Tick two or more samples and choose Compare samples to see their taxonomy side by side.

Taxonomy sunburst

Rings run from the current level outwards; segment angle is proportional to read count. Colour identifies the domain (Bacteria, Archaea, Viruses). Select a segment, or an entry in the list under the chart, to restrict the table to that group. The breadcrumb above the chart moves back up. The list is the keyboard route through the same hierarchy.

Table

One row per reference genome in the selected sample. The damage column shows the value, a bar and a tick at the damage threshold. Column headers sort the table. The filters above it restrict rows by status, by text (species, genus, family or accession), and by minimum damage, significance and read count. Export CSV downloads the rows currently shown.

Taxon details

Selecting a row opens its smiley plot with the damage fit, read and alignment statistics, coverage statistics and lineage. Full view opens a larger plot with the plotted values. Species across samples and Genus across samples show the same taxon in every sample, ordered by age.

Comparison

Tick rows in the table, or use Add to comparison, then choose Compare taxa to see their plots and metrics together. The selection can be exported as CSV.

Search

Search looks across all samples for a species, genus, family or reference accession and opens the chosen genome in its sample.

URL parameters

The explorer keeps its state in the query string of explorer/, so any view can be bookmarked or shared. Copy link copies the current address. Parameters at their default are left out.

ParameterValueExample
sampleSample nameGeoB15103-1_19-34cm
taxonReference accession of the open genomeGCF_000759875.1
pathTaxonomy filter, ranks separated by / with their prefixesd__Bacteria/p__Cyanobacteria
statusdamaged or non-damageddamaged
qText filterProchlorococcus
min_damageMinimum damage, 0–10.2
min_significanceMinimum significance3
min_readsMinimum read count1000
sortColumn and direction. Columns: species, family, damage, significance, n_readsn_reads:desc
compareSamples ticked for comparison, comma-separatedGeoB15103-1_19-34cm,GeoB15103-2_21-34cm

Example: explorer/?sample=GeoB15103-1_19-34cm&path=d__Bacteria&status=damaged&sort=n_reads:desc

A parameter that does not match the data is ignored and reported in a message.

Data files

The files the explorer reads can be downloaded and queried directly: meta.parquet, freq.parquet, samples.json and config.json. Columns are described in data/README.md and data/schema.json; llms.txt is a plain-text summary for scripts and AI agents.

Data build: see config.json (build_id ).
